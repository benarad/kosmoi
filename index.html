Tilt of the fourth sphere to the third

Aristotle says only that the fourth sphere is slanted to the third, and no source records the angle. The figure of eight that the planet traces (the hippopede) is twice as long as the tilt.

Where each number comes from

    Sizes and distances are not to scale. Aristotle gives no figures for them, and his spheres are in contact with one another, with no gaps between them.

    The fixed stars are the stars of the Yale Bright Star Catalogue down to magnitude 4.6, about a thousand in all, placed where they were in 350 BC. Athens is taken to be at 38° north, and the zodiac to be slanted at 24° to the equator.
